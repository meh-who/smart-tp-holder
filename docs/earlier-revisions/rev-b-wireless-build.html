Riding the top of the roll is the right call

It's better than what I proposed, and worth being precise about why, because it looks like it shouldn't work.

A wheel resting on the roll doesn't measure angle — it measures surface travel. The paper leaving the roll and the surface passing under the wheel are the same material moving at the same speed. So the wheel turns once per π × 21 mm of paper no matter how fat the roll is. The diameter cancels out of the equation completely.

What it costs, and these are real

  • The arm needs 33 mm of sprung travel. The contact point drops from r=55 mm to r=22 mm over one roll. A 70 mm follower arm sweeps that in about 40°, from 65° to 27° off vertical. Short arms won't reach the core; that's the number that sizes the mechanism.
  • It counts roll spin, not hand pulls. Yank hard and the roll overspins, dumping slack the wheel dutifully measures. That paper genuinely left the roll, so it isn't wrong exactly — but it's why max_queue: 4 and max_backlog_s: 20 matter more in this version than the last.
  • Contact pressure is the tuning knob. Too light and it slips and under-counts; too heavy and it compresses the outer layers and tears the sheet at the perforation instead of turning. Start light, add tension until three calibration runs agree within 3%.
  • Resolution drops to 2.75 mm per count. Fine for 114 mm sheets, but the old 3 mm noise floor was under one count — a single spurious edge would have fired a headline. Default is now 6 mm (two counts).

Spec the 2 A boost, not the 1 A

I costed the 1 A module first and it doesn't survive the arithmetic. The Pi peaks around 600 mA on a WiFi transmit burst; the MAX98357A driving 4 Ω at full tilt pulls about 950 mA. Worst case those coincide at 1.55 A. The 1000 µF bulk cap only rides out about 0.47 ms before sagging — enough for a switching transient, nowhere near enough for a loud sentence. Either fit a 2 A module, or keep volume ≤ 0.8 and accept brownout risk. The 2 A module costs a few dollars more; take the module.

Why 10 kΩ + 10 nF and not 10 kΩ + 100 nF

A hard yank moves paper at roughly 1 m/s, which at 2.75 mm/count is 364 counts/s — a 2.7 ms period, so 1.4 ms per half-cycle. A 10 kΩ/100 nF network has a 1 ms time constant and would smear that into mush. 10 kΩ + 10 nF gives 100 µs, roughly 14× faster than the fastest edge you'll ever see, while still killing contact bounce. If you're using an optical encoder you can skip the RC entirely.

The thing you should know before buying the battery

A Pi Zero 2 W has no usable sleep state, so that 115 mA floor runs 24 hours a day whether anyone uses the holder or not. Every software trick combined buys about 30%, not 10×. That's why the runtime ceiling is days, not months.

An ESP32-S3 doing the same job — deep sleep at ~18 µA, woken by the encoder edge, WiFi up for four seconds every half hour — comes to about 12.6 mAh/day. A small 2,000 mAh cell would then last five months, in a package a fraction of the size.

My suggestion: build it on the Pi with a 20,000 mAh pack and live with charging it on Sundays. The interaction is what you're still designing, and Python on a real Linux box is the right place to be iterating. Port to the ESP32 once you've stopped changing your mind about how it should feel — geometry.py and motion.py translate to C++ almost line for line, and they're the only parts with any subtlety in them.

Bring-up
04

Assembly order

Build it in this order. Each step is testable on its own, which matters because debugging all of it at once is miserable.

  1. Pi on USB power, no peripherals. Flash Raspberry Pi OS Lite 64-bit, get it on WiFi, run sudo bash hardware/setup-pi.sh, reboot.
  2. Audio only. Wire the amp and speaker. aplay -l must show a hifiberry card; speaker-test -c1 -t sine -f 440 -l1 must make a noise. Nothing else works until this does.
  3. Encoder on the bench, wheel in your fingers. Wire A/B/common with the RC network. Spin it by hand and watch the count move both ways: 
    # must go up AND down
python3 -c "from gpiozero import RotaryEncoder; import time; \
e=RotaryEncoder(5,6,max_steps=0); \
[print(e.steps) or time.sleep(.2) for _ in range(50)]"
  4. Power chain, still on the bench. Battery into the charger, switch, 5 V out measured with a meter before it touches the Pi. Confirm it holds ≥4.9 V while the amp plays loudly. This is where a 1 A module fails.
  5. Mechanical assembly. Arm, spring, wheel on the roll. Check the wheel still contacts a nearly-empty roll — wind one down or shim a 44 mm core to test it.
  6. Calibrate. Three runs of tools/calibrate.py, paste mm_per_count, confirm the spread is under 3%.
  7. Set sensor.kind: quadrature in the config and start the service. If pulling paper makes the count go down, set invert: true rather than swapping wires.

What changed in the code

  • display.py is gone. Along with SPI, Pillow, the Waveshare vendoring step and the draw thread. The low-paper readout it used to show is now spoken instead, once, below 12 sheets.
  • power.py is new. Amp shutdown control and the low-battery watchdog, which warns out loud then halts cleanly after five minutes — a Pi that browns out mid-write corrupts its SD card, and nobody is watching this one.
  • The loop idles down. 200 Hz while paper moves, 40 Hz after eight seconds of quiet.
  • noise_floor_mm is 6.0, not 3.0. Two counts at scroll-wheel resolution instead of one. There's a test asserting this stays true if you change the wheel.
  • 21 tests now, up from 16. The new ones cover the surface-wheel geometry at five roll diameters and the amp's wake/release logic.
