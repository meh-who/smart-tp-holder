Assembly guide · revision 2.0

The holder, with an NFC tag and a wall plug

Everything that changed for the exhibition version: the holder runs from mains, stays online, and a visitor taps it with a phone to choose what it reads. The mechanical build (follower arm, declicked encoder, speaker box) is unchanged from the sensor-mounting document, which is included alongside.

0 · What changed, and why

Before (rev C)Now (rev 2.0)Why
2000 mAh LiPo, light sleep, hourly sync5 V USB wall adapter, always on, polls the server every 20 sThe gallery has an outlet; the visitor's source switch has to land within seconds, not an hour.
Source set by editing the serverVisitor taps an NFC tag → companion web page → picks News / X / TikTok / ReelsThe visitor does the switching, so the news-versus-reels contrast is something they trigger.
No telemetryHeartbeat every 20 s: online, last pull, items cached, sheets leftThe page shows connection status, and you can see from your phone whether the piece is alive.

You need an NFC tag, not an NFC reader

The phone is the reader. A passive sticker holding a URL is all the holder needs; iPhone and Android both pop up the link when tapped, with no app. A PN532 reader module would be for the holder reading the phone, which iPhones won't do for arbitrary apps. Nothing in the firmware knows the tag exists.

Avoid pin 12 for anything with a pull-up (the board won't boot), and A2/A3/A4/A13 (input-only). The Feather's header has only one GND pin on the long side, so make a ground bus on the perma-proto and run everything to it.

3 · Assembly, in order

  1. Headers. Solder the long header strip onto the HUZZAH32 and the 7-pin header plus the screw terminal onto one MAX98357A. Push the headers into a breadboard first so they solder straight.
  2. Bench test 1: sound. On a breadboard, wire the amp only (VIN→USB, GND, BCLK→A0, LRC→A1, DIN→A5, SD→MO) and the speaker. Flash firmware/bringup/bringup.ino. You should hear a two-note chime. Fix this before going further.
  3. Bench test 2: LED and button. Add the LED (21 → 330 Ω → long leg, short leg → GND) and the button (27 ↔ GND). Reset: the LED breathes, flashes twice, and the Serial Monitor prints “button down / up”.
  4. Declick the encoder. Open the can's tabs, lift the detent spring out, close. Flick test: the rotor must coast, not stop at the next click. Do several; keep the smoothest.
  5. Fit the G403 wheel. Its shaft has a flat-sided end and a round end. Push the flat end into the encoder rotor: it should key snugly and turn the rotor without slipping. If it's loose, a thin sleeve of heat-shrink on the shaft end or a drop of thin CA fixes it. Measure the round end with calipers — that decides the bearing (parts list).
  6. Encoder board. Solder the encoder to a scrap of perfboard; 28–30 AWG silicone wires (A, C, B) as a twisted bundle to a 3-pin JST-PH. Put the two 10 kΩ / 10 nF pairs on the main board end, not at the encoder.
  7. Bench test 3: encoder. Plug it in, run bringup again, turn the wheel: counts scroll in the Serial Monitor and the speaker ticks. Dragging paper forward must count up; if it counts down, swap A and B.
  8. Move to perma-proto. Same wiring, soldered. The amp sits under the speaker; keep the encoder pair away from the speaker leads. Plug the speaker in with a 2-pin JST pigtail into the screw terminal.
  9. Follower arm. Encoder board on the arm, wheel riding the top of the roll, pivot 75 mm behind and 60 mm above the spindle, light spring (5–15 gf). Geometry is in sensor-mounting.html. Dry-fit on a full roll and an empty core.
  10. Enclosure. Speaker against a grille with a sealed back, LED behind a 3 mm hole at the top edge, NFC sticker under the recessed top, ≥ 30 mm from the speaker magnet.
  11. Flash the real firmware (§5) and calibrate.

4 · The NFC tag

Where

On the front face of the holder's cover, centred, where a thumb naturally rests when reaching for the roll. Phones read through 3–4 mm of plastic or wood; not through metal. If the cover is metal, use an on-metal (ferrite-backed) tag on the outside and let it show — it reads as an invitation.

Keep it ≥ 30 mm from the encoder can and the speaker magnet. Put a second tag on the wall label as a backup.

What to write

One NDEF URL record: https://<your server>/r/<HOLDER_ID> — the same id as HOLDER_ID in config.h. Nothing else on the tag.

Write it with the free NFC Tools app (iOS/Android): Write → Add a record → URL → Write. Then lock the tag (Other → Lock tag) so a visitor cannot overwrite it. Locking is permanent, so test first.

iPhones read tags with the screen on, from the top edge; Androids from the middle of the back. Mention both on the wall label: “Hold your phone to the cover.”

5 · Flash the firmware

  1. Arduino IDE 2.x, Boards Manager → esp32 by Espressif (3.x).
  2. Libraries (Library Manager): ESP32Encoder, ESP8266Audio, ArduinoJson (7.x).
  3. Board settings: Tools → Board → esp32 → Adafruit ESP32 Feather (or “ESP32 Dev Module”) · Partition Scheme: No OTA (2MB APP/2MB SPIFFS) — the default leaves too little room for the voice clips · Upload Speed 921600 · Port: the one that appears when you plug the board in (if none appears, the cable is charge-only).
  4. Edit config.h: HOLDER_ID, HOLDER_KEY (from python manage.py key), WIFI_SSID/PASS, SERVER_URL. Leave MAINS_POWERED 1.
  5. Upload, open Serial Monitor at 115200. You should see the IP, then [sync] bulletin …: N items from news within half a minute.
  6. Pull a sheet. It speaks. If the count runs backwards (status shows travel not increasing), set ENCODER_INVERT 1 or swap A and B.

Calibrate once

Type cal in the serial console to mark the start, pull exactly five sheets (570 mm on standard perforation), tear, then type cal 570. It stores mm-per-count in flash. Check with status; a three-sheet pull should log three items.

6 · Deploy the server

Anything that runs Docker and has a public HTTPS hostname: a €5 VPS, Fly.io, Railway, a Pi at home behind a tunnel. HTTPS is required — the OAuth providers refuse plain-HTTP callbacks, and iOS only opens https links from tags without a warning.

# on the server
git clone … holder && cd holder/server
cp .env.example .env            # set PUBLIC_URL, SECRET_KEY, TTS_PROVIDER, feeds, OAuth keys
docker build -t holder .
docker run -d --name holder --env-file .env -p 8000:8000 -v holder-data:/data --restart unless-stopped holder
# put Caddy/nginx/Cloudflare in front for TLS on PUBLIC_URL

# register the holder (same id and key as config.h) and warm the cache
docker exec holder python manage.py add-holder lisbon-01 <key> --name "TEI Lisbon"
docker exec holder python manage.py build lisbon-01 news
docker exec holder python manage.py tag-url lisbon-01     # → write this to the tag

Text to speech

TTS_PROVIDER=edge is free and sounds good (Microsoft neural voices through edge-tts). openai needs a key and sounds better. silent produces quiet tones of the right length so you can test the whole pipeline on a laptop with no network. Clips are cached by content hash in /data/media, so re-reads cost nothing.

Plan B for the opening: curated sets

If an app review has not come through, drop /data/curated/tiktok.json (or reels.json, x.json) on the server — {"items":[{"id":"1","text":"…"}]}. The page then shows that service as “Connected · curated set” and the switch works exactly the same. Say so on the wall text.

7 · Commissioning on site ≈ 90 min, in this order

  • ☐ Wall bracket level at 700 mm; USB cable dressed; adapter plugged in; LED on.
  • ☐ Serial console (or the page) shows Online within 60 s on the venue wi-fi. If the venue has a captive portal, use your own hotspot or a travel router — the ESP32 cannot click “Accept”.
  • ☐ Tap tag with an iPhone and an Android. Page opens, shows Online, Now reading: News.
  • ☐ LED: breathing on power-up, steady once the page shows Online, a double flash when you tap the tag.
  • ☐ Pull three sheets → three items, in order, ~13 s of speech. Backlog: pull a metre → stops after four items.
  • ☐ Switch to a second source on the page → “Preparing…” → within 20 s the holder says “Now reading TikTok.” and the next sheet is from it.
  • ☐ Hold the button 2 s → “New roll.” Page shows 200 sheets left.
  • ☐ Unplug the router for a minute: holder keeps reading its cache; page shows Offline, then recovers.
  • ☐ Volume check from the doorway and from the seat. Hardware: GAIN open = 9 dB, GAIN to GND = 12 dB; GAIN_DB in config.h trims in software.
  • ☐ Tray under the holder; first roll fitted; spare rolls and the second tag in the drawer.

8 · If something is wrong

SymptomLook at
Page says “Not heard from yet”Wrong SERVER_URL or HOLDER_KEY (server log shows 401), or wi-fi. Serial console tells you which.
Speaks only on the first pull after boot, then silenceAmp SD wiring (pin MO) or the 5 V rail sagging — check the 470 µF, use a real 2 A adapter.
Items fire on wobble or not at allCalibration (status → mm/count), debounce caps, or the tyre skidding: more spring preload, cleaner O-ring.
Switching takes minutesTTS is slow on first build (edge ≈ 1 s per item). Warm the cache with manage.py build for every source before opening.
Phone opens the page but buttons say “open the page from the holder's tag”Cookies blocked (private browsing). Normal browsing mode, or re-tap.
Tag reads nothingMetal behind it. Use an on-metal tag, or move it to the plastic part of the cover.
Crackle during downloadShouldn't happen (sync runs on the other core), but if the board has no PSRAM, lower POLL_MAINS_S traffic by setting it to 30.
