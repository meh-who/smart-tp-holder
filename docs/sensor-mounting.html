Mounting the encoder

The sensor doesn't attach to the holder — it rides on a sprung arm that follows the roll down as it empties. Four drawings: where the arm goes, how the wheel fits the encoder, what the contact has to do, and how it wires back.

1 · Where the arm goes
Why this pivot and not another

The arm presses the wheel along the line back to the roll's centre, and the leverage it has over that line is what sets the contact force. With this placement the leverage changes by only 1.06× between a full roll and a bare core — the geometry very nearly compensates for itself, which is the whole reason to put the pivot back and up rather than directly above.

So the spring is the thing to get right

Since the geometry is near-neutral, almost all the force variation comes from the spring winding up as the arm drops. A torsion spring preloaded only 20° past its free angle gives 2.3× more force at the core than at a full roll — it will grip at the end of a roll and slip at the start, which looks exactly like a broken sensor.

Preload it at least 90° and the force stays inside 1.33× end to end. A long spring wound well past the 24° sweep, not a short stiff one barely engaged.

Build order

The sequence that avoids rework

  • Declick first, before anything else is made. Flick test: a declicked rotor coasts, a detented one stops dead. If you can't get a clean declick, the whole arm geometry changes and you want to know now.
  • Measure the hex across the flats with calipers, then model the hub. It may differ between heights in the pack, so measure the specific 13 mm pieces you'll use.
  • Print the hub and test the fit dry — no O-ring, no arm. It should press on firmly enough not to slip on the hex but come off without destroying the rotor.
  • Solder the encoder to perfboard and verify counts on the bench, wheel in your fingers, before it ever goes near the arm. The count must move both up and down.
  • Then build the arm, and set the spring so the wheel rolls under the lightest pressure that doesn't skid. Light enough to leave no mark on the paper.
  • Calibrate last. Three runs; if they disagree by more than 3%, the wheel is slipping and the spring needs more preload — not more force.
The failure you'll actually see

Slip reads short, never long. If the device consistently gives you fewer headlines than sheets pulled, the wheel is skidding — more preload, or check the declick. If it gives you more, that's not slip, that's the roll overspinning on a hard yank, and max_queue is the fix.
